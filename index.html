Research

The question I keep coming back to: how do atomic structure and interfacial bonding set the mechanical response we measure at the bulk scale? I follow it from DFT and MD up to FEM, from zeolite frameworks to 3D-printed metamaterials, and from molecular bonding at a polymer/inorganic interface to the adhesion strength of a coating.

Right now

  • Polymer / inorganic interface adhesionMolecular simulation of how polymers stick to inorganic surfaces (industry-sponsored)
  • Interpretable potentialsInteratomic potentials whose energy terms can be read and checked, not only fitted
  • Ice on icephobic surfacesMD of ice adhesion and slippage on PDMS-based icephobic surfaces (KSME 2025/2026)
  • ML potentials for alloysMachine-learned interatomic potentials for metals, moving toward multi-element alloys

Evaluating Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials for Accurate and Scalable Modeling of Organometallic Precursors

Seungpyo Kang*, JunHo Song*, Jinyoung Jeong, Taehyun Park, Joonghee Won, Jungim Han, Kyoungmin Min

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 17(41), 57226–57239 (2025) [1]

Machine-learned potentials for organometallic precursors

  • Question: which machine-learned potential gives both accuracy and scale for molecular crystals of ALD precursors?
  • 3 potentials compared: MTP, CHGNet, SevenNet (cyclopentadienyl precursors of Zr, Hf, Sr, Ba)
  • SevenNet best: energy MAE 0.001 eV/atom, force R² 1.000 (CUFBAG)
  • Melting points from solid–liquid coexistence MD: 0.65–2.05% off experiment (CUFBAG), 1.87–7.58% for the rest
  • Scale: MTP runs ~35,000 atoms at 1.253 ns/day; SevenNet up to 7,500 atoms

Co-first author

Investigating the compressive behavior of zeolite-based porous mechanical metamaterials

Dosung Lee*, JunHo Song*, Kyoungmin Min, Namjung Kim

Materials & Design 249, 113541 (2025) [2]

Zeolite crystals, printed as soft metamaterials

My part: Writing – original draft, Software, Methodology, Investigation, Formal analysis, Data curation

  • Question: can an atomic crystal structure become a macro-scale metamaterial? Zeolite frameworks go from the atomistic unit cell to a 3D-printed part (micro to macro)
  • 21 unit cells chosen from ~590,000 zeolites (isotropy, printability) → supercells → electron-density surfaces
  • Printed by stereolithography in a hyperelastic resin; FEM (Yeoh model) to 50% strain + compression tests with DIC
  • 4 response groups: nonlinear, linear, bilinear, collapsed
  • Best group on average: 0.403 MPa modulus, 139,498 J/m³ absorbed
  • Mechanical metamaterials
  • Zeolites
  • 3D printing
  • FEM
  • Micro to macro

Co-first author

Molecular dynamics and machine learning insights into the mechanical behavior of zeolites under large deformation

JunHo Song*, Dosung Lee*, Namjung Kim, Kyoungmin Min

Materials Today Communications 41, 110922 (2024) [3]

Squeezing zeolites to 95% strain with MD and ML

My part: Writing – original draft, Validation, Methodology, Formal analysis

  • Question: how do zeolites behave under large compression? Mostly studied only in the elastic range. Our earlier work showed that ductile 3D-printed zeolites follow MD up to ~30% strain, so this is the atomistic end of a micro-to-macro line
  • ReaxFF MD: compression of 2,710 zeolites up to 95% strain
  • ML screen: 39,589 near-isotropic candidates out of 590,448 hypothetical frameworks; top 50 + bottom 50 checked by MD
  • Best ML-picked zeolite absorbs ~20% more energy than the best of the 2,710
  • Energy absorption tracks compressive stress (R = 0.74); the Si–O–Si angle matters, Si–O bond length barely does
  • Molecular dynamics
  • ReaxFF
  • Zeolites
  • Large deformation
  • ML screening

Enhancing protein aggregation prediction: a unified analysis leveraging graph convolutional networks and active learning

Jiwon Sun*, JunHo Song*, Juo Kim*, Seungpyo Kang, Eunyoung Park, Seung-woo Seo, Kyoungmin Min

RSC Advances 14, 31439–31450 (2024) [4]

Predicting protein aggregation with graphs

  • Question: most aggregation predictors read the sequence only. Can a 3D-structure graph do better, and faster?
  • Graph convolutional network trained on 302,032 PDB entries, tested on 21,873 human AlphaFold2 structures
  • R² 0.9849, MAE 0.0381; about 42,480× faster than AGGRESCAN3D
  • Active learning (expected improvement): 541 of 546 high-aggregation proteins (99%) by iteration 20, MAE 0.0291
  • Graph neural networks
  • Protein aggregation
  • AlphaFold2
  • Active learning
  • Structure-based ML

Co-first author

Interpretable machine learning boosting the discovery of targeted organometallic compounds with optimal bandgap

Taehyun Park*, JunHo Song*, Jinyoung Jeong, Seungpyo Kang, Joonchul Kim, Joonghee Won, Jungim Han, Kyoungmin Min

Materials Today Advances 23, 100520 (2024) [5]

Which features set an organometallic band gap?

My part: Writing – original draft, Validation, Methodology, Formal analysis

  • Question: organometallics lack band-gap data, and graph models predict well but explain little. Which few features matter?
  • High-throughput DFT on >18,000 organometallics (plus ~12,000 organics from OMDB-GAP1)
  • >3,500 descriptors narrowed to 48 key features (about 1%): R² drops only ~0.01, MAE rises only ~0.01 eV
  • Active learning toward the 1.34 eV solar-cell gap: expected improvement finds 69% of targets after searching 46% of the space
  • DFT

  • Interpretable ML
  • Active learning

Correlating atomistic characteristics of zeolites to their 3D-Printed Macro structural properties for prediction of mechanical response

Joonchul Kim, Minseong Hong, JunHo Song, Namjung Kim, Kyoungmin Min

Materials & Design 233, 112189 (2023) [12]

Zeolites from atoms to 3D-printed lattices

  • 21 cubic zeolites from the IZA database: ReaxFF MD on 19, then 3D-printed 10 of them in PLA and TPU (micro to macro)
  • Brittle PLA prints break at 5–10% strain; ductile TPU prints follow MD up to about 30% strain
  • Density and porosity correlate strongly with stress and modulus
  • Angle energy dominates up to 30% strain; bond energy takes over above 40%
